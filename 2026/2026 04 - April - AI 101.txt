I mentioned at one time, I have been trying to learn AI.

What is intelligence?

	- Dictionary says it is an ability to acquire and apply knowledge and skills.

What is artificial?
	
	- Dictionary says it is something that is not genuine, not natural, or something that is fake or simulated.


AI has advanced so much it feels like it will take away all the jobs, do all the work and eventually take over the world.

But, what is AI?

ChatGPT came into existence in late 2022. 
Then, the AI options exploded.

However, all of us have been using AI apps for more than a decade.
For example - Apple Siri, Amazon Alexa, Google Translate, Netflix/YouTube recommendations, Spotify Recommendations etc.

**Title: “Artificial Intelligence… Real Confusion”**

Good evening,

Let me start with a confession.

A few months ago, I confidently explained AI to my 15-year-old.

I said, “AI is the future. It’s going to change everything.”

He looked at me and said,
“Dad… it already has. You just discovered it.”

That was the moment I realized…
I’m not ahead of the curve.
I’m being *dragged* by it.

---

So let’s start simple.

**What is Artificial Intelligence?**

AI is the ability of machines to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence—
like understanding language, recognizing patterns, making decisions.

In short…

> AI is a machine trying to act like a human.

Which is ironic…

Because most of us are still trying to act like functional humans.

---

Now here’s the funny part.

We think AI is new.

It’s not.

We’ve been using AI for over a decade—just without calling it “AI.”

* When Netflix recommended your next show… that was AI.
* When Google finished your sentence… that was AI.
* When your GPS said, “recalculating”… judging your life choices… that was definitely AI.

And let’s be honest…

Netflix knows you better than your family.

It doesn’t ask, “How was your day?”
It says, “You watched 6 episodes… are you okay?”

---

Now let’s go one level deeper.

**What is a model?**

A model is the brain of AI.

It’s a system trained on massive amounts of data
to recognize patterns and make predictions.

Think of it like this:

A model is like a student
who has read the entire internet…

but has no life experience.

So it sounds very confident…

…but sometimes says things that make no sense.

Kind of like… some people on social media.

---

Which brings us to the next question:

**How do we train a model?**

There are three main ways—and honestly, training a model is a lot like training a puppy.

1. **Supervised learning**
   You show the model the right answers again and again.
   “Sit.” Reward.
   “Stay.” Reward.

   Like a puppy learning commands… with treats.

2. **Unsupervised learning**
   You just give it a bunch of data… and let it figure things out.

   Like leaving a puppy alone in the house…
   and hoping it learns what not to chew.

   Spoiler: it won’t.

3. **Reinforcement learning**
   You reward good behavior and discourage bad behavior.

   Sit? Treat.
   Chew the couch? No treat.

   Basically…

   how we train puppies…
   how we train kids…
   and honestly… how companies train employees.

---

Now here’s where things get interesting.

**What is hallucination?**

In AI, hallucination means the system makes things up.

Confidently.

Completely wrong…

…but beautifully explained.

Basically, like that one friend we all have.

You know the one.

Always boasting.

Always bluffing.

“I’ve been there.”
“I know that guy.”
“I read that book.”

You ask one follow-up question…

…and suddenly the story gets… creative.

I once asked an AI for a reference.

It gave me a perfect answer.

Author name.
Book title.
Publication year.

Everything looked real.

There was just one small problem…

None of it existed.

It created a fake expert… with a fake book…
and I almost quoted it in a meeting.

That’s when I realized…

AI doesn’t just answer questions.

It improvises.

It’s basically… a very fast, very confident storyteller.

---

And finally…

**What is bias?**

Bias is when AI reflects the patterns—and prejudices—of the data it was trained on.

Think of it like that one uncle at family gatherings…

You know the one.

He says, “I’m not biased, I’m just telling it like it is.”

And everyone else is like, “No, Uncle… you’re just repeating things you heard in 1985.”

AI works the same way.

If you train it on biased data, it doesn’t question it… it just repeats it—faster, louder, and with better grammar.

If the data is biased…

the AI will be biased.

For example:

Ask AI to generate an image of a CEO…

and historically, it often showed a certain type of person.

Not because AI is opinionated…

but because it learned from us.

So when we say AI has bias…

what we’re really saying is…

AI is holding up a mirror.

And sometimes…

we don’t like what we see.

---

So where does that leave us?

AI can learn.
AI can predict.
AI can even sound intelligent.

But it doesn’t *understand*.

It doesn’t have judgment.
It doesn’t have values.
It doesn’t know when it’s wrong.

Which is… honestly… still a problem humans haven’t fully solved either.

---

Let me end with this.

AI is powerful.

It can write emails, generate ideas, even help us prepare speeches like this one.

But intelligence is not just about giving answers.

It’s about asking better questions.

It’s about judgment.

It’s about responsibility.

So the real question is not:

“Is AI intelligent?”

The real question is:

> “Are we intelligent enough… to use it wisely?”

Because in the end…

AI will not replace us.

But humans… using AI…

will replace humans who don’t.

Thank you.
