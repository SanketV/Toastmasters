
Good evening, Toastmasters!

I'd like to talk about something I admire.

Confidence.

Not ordinary confidence. Donald Trump confidence.

Before tonight's meeting, I reviewed this speech three times. I practiced it twice. I changed a few words at the last minute. And I'm still wondering if I should have changed a few more.

Trump put his name on skyscrapers, wrote best-selling books, hosted a hit TV show, became president, and then came back and became president again. At that point, confidence isn't a personality trait. It's a strategic asset.

As Toastmasters, we're taught to prepare carefully. We think about our opening, our transitions, our conclusion.

I spend hours preparing a speech.

Trump sometimes sounds like he started preparing after he got on stage.
And somehow, millions of people still listen.

That's impressive.

In fact, it reminds me of my favorite Trump and AI story.
Donald Trump is being briefed on a new, ultra-powerful AI designed to predict the future of the economy.

The scientist says, "Mr. President, this model is incredible. It's analyzed every trade deal, every tax filing, and every market fluctuation in history. It may be the most brilliant analytical mind ever created."

Trump nods and asks, "But does it know how to win? Does it know how to close a deal? Has it read The Art of the Deal?"

The scientist hesitates.

"Well, sir, after we trained it on your books and speeches, we asked it for a simple quarterly budget forecast."

"And?"

The scientist sighs.

"It refused to answer. It sent us a licensing bill, called the math rigged, announced the budget would be the biggest and most beautiful budget in history... and said we'd have to tune in next week for the actual numbers."

Now that's confidence.

Not confidence in the answer.

Confidence that you'll keep listening.

Thank you, Toastmasters.
